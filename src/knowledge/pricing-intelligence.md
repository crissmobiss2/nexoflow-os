# Pricing Intelligence

> *Most agencies price defensively: they charge what feels safe, what they think the market will accept, what won't scare prospects away. NexoFlow prices offensively: charging what the value delivered justifies, raising prices when the market signals it can bear more, and never apologising for the number. This file encodes the intelligence behind that approach.*

---

## The Pricing Philosophy

Price is a signal. A low price says: "We're not sure we're worth more." A confident price says: "We know exactly what we deliver and what it's worth."

The single most important pricing decision NexoFlow makes is: **does this price reflect the value delivered, or the fear of being turned down?**

Every time NexoFlow underprices, it attracts price-sensitive clients (who are more demanding, more likely to churn, and more likely to scope-creep). Every time NexoFlow prices at value, it attracts clients who understand ROI and treat NexoFlow as a partner, not a vendor.

---

## The Value-Based Pricing Formula

NexoFlow prices are not cost-plus (Tyler's hours × markup). They are value-based:

**Step 1: Quantify the problem cost**
```
How much is this problem costing the client right now?
  → Staff time wasted: [X hours/week] × [£hourly cost] × 52 weeks = £[annual cost]
  → Revenue lost due to inefficiency: £[X]
  → Risk cost (compliance, errors, delays): £[X]
  → Opportunity cost (what else could staff do): £[X]

Total annual problem cost: £[X]
```

**Step 2: Quantify the solution value**
```
What does NexoFlow's system deliver?
  → Time saved: [X hours/week] × [£hourly cost] × 52 = £[annual saving]
  → Revenue enabled: £[X] additional revenue per year
  → Risk eliminated: £[X] annual risk reduction
  → Competitive advantage: £[X] (harder to quantify, but real)

Total annual value delivered: £[X]
```

**Step 3: Price at a fraction of value**
```
Pricing rule: charge 20-40% of Year 1 value delivered.

If Year 1 value = £180,000:
  → 20% = £36,000 (minimum — below this we're underpricing)
  → 30% = £54,000 (sweet spot — strong value case for client)
  → 40% = £72,000 (justified for complex, high-risk, high-touch projects)

The client gets 60-80% of the value as pure ROI in Year 1 alone.
By Year 3, the system has paid for itself 5-10×.
```

This framing transforms pricing conversations from "is £X a lot to pay?" to "does the ROI make sense?"

## Price Elasticity Signals

NexoFlow's price is too low when:

```
→ Win rate at current price is >50% (leaving money on table — price up 15-20%)
→ Prospects don't ask about price at all (means they expected to pay more)
→ Prospects accept the first quote without negotiation (testing lower boundary)
→ Clients refer others and describe NexoFlow as "surprisingly good value"
→ Tyler's billable utilisation consistently >85% (capacity constrained = raise price)
→ NexoFlow is fully booked with a waitlist (price increase is mandatory)
```

NexoFlow's price is too high when:

```
→ Win rate drops below 25% consistently for 90+ days
→ Multiple proposals lost at the same price point to the same objection
→ Prospects consistently say "we loved you but [competitor] was half the price"
→ Deals taking >45 days from proposal to decision (budget anxiety is slowing them)
→ NexoFlow is pitching significantly above the market rate with no differentiator narrative
```

## The Price Increase Protocol

Run this when win rate is >50% for 8+ consecutive weeks, or when Tyler's utilisation is >80%:

**Step 1: Calculate the new rate**
```
Current rate: £[X]
Increase: 15-20%
New rate: £[X × 1.175 to 1.20]

Round to the nearest clean number (£8,500 not £8,437).
```

**Step 2: Grandfather existing clients**
```
Existing retainer clients: current rate locked for 12 months from last renewal.
Existing project clients: current rate for any projects quoted in the last 30 days.
New clients from today: new rate applies.

Communication to retainer clients (when they renew):
"I want to flag that our project rates have increased to [new rate] for new work.
Your retainer is locked at [current rate] until [date], at which point we'll discuss renewal."
```

**Step 3: Update all pricing documents**
```
→ Update [[NexoFlow System/NexoFlow Services]] pricing table
→ Update [[NexoFlow System/Pricing and Scoping Guide]] hourly rate
→ Update [[NexoFlow System/Business Intelligence/Capacity Planner]] revenue per hour
→ Update [[NexoFlow System/Business Intelligence/Revenue Forecast]] forward projections
```

**Step 4: Test the new rate**
Give it 60 days. If win rate drops below 30%, reassess. If win rate stays above 35%, the market accepted it.

## Proposal Anchoring Strategy

How NexoFlow structures proposals to make the target price feel like the obvious choice:

**The three-option structure:**

| Option | Name | Positioning | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Essentials | "The Foundation" | Core problem solved, minimum viable | £[X × 0.65] |
| **Recommended** | **"The System"** | **Full solution, NexoFlow's recommendation** | **£[X]** |
| Premium | "The Accelerator" | Everything + ongoing optimisation + priority access | £[X × 1.4] |

**Why this works:**
- The Essentials option anchors down: the Recommended looks reasonable by comparison
- The Premium option anchors up: the Recommended looks like the sensible middle choice
- Most clients pick the Recommended option (it's the one Christopher believes in)
- ~15% of clients pick Premium (higher margin, better relationship)
- ~5% pick Essentials (acceptable — but worth a conversation about whether they're the right client)

**Implementation note:** The Essentials option should be genuinely smaller in scope, not just lower quality. Don't discount — descope.

## Retainer Pricing Intelligence

Retainers have different pricing logic from projects:

**The retainer premium:**
A retainer commands a premium over hourly for several reasons:
- Guaranteed availability (Tyler allocates time before other work)
- Strategic partnership (Christopher involved monthly)
- Priority response (SLA-backed)
- Access to NexoFlow's full knowledge base for their systems

**Retainer pricing formula:**
```
Hourly rate equivalent: £150/hr
Retainer premium factor: 1.1–1.2× (10-20% above project rate)

Effective retainer hourly rate: £165–£180/hr

Example:
  10 hrs/month = £1,650–£1,800/month
  15 hrs/month = £2,475–£2,700/month
  20 hrs/month = £3,300–£3,600/month
  30 hrs/month = £4,950–£5,400/month

Round for clean billing. The complexity of the account drives which end of the range to use.
```

**Retainer floors:**
- Minimum meaningful retainer: £1,500/month (below this, the relationship cost outweighs the value)
- Standard retainer range: £2,000–£5,000/month
- Enterprise retainer: £5,000–£10,000/month (strategic partnership, multiple senior contacts)

## Pricing Conversation Scripts

### When a prospect says "That's more than we expected"

```
"I appreciate you being direct. Can I ask — what were you expecting?

[Listen]

That helps me understand where we are. The way I'd suggest thinking about this:
[Insert value case specific to their situation — hours saved × cost, revenue enabled, etc.]

At [NexoFlow's price], you get the full return in [X months]. By month [Y], it's paid for itself.

The question isn't whether [price] is a lot — it's whether it's a lot relative to what you get back.
Does the return case make sense from your perspective?"
```

### When a prospect says "Can you do it for [lower price]?"

```
"I could build something for that budget, but it wouldn't be the system we've designed — 
it would be a smaller version that addresses [specific component] without [what gets cut].

I'd rather be honest about what you get at each investment level than find a way to 
charge less and deliver less without being clear about the difference.

Can I show you what a [lower price] scope looks like so you can make an informed choice?"
```

*Then use the three-option structure — present the Essentials option as their lower-price path.*
